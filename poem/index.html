The Limbo
I was good in codes.
Good in my little world of Python,
where errors had reasons
and broken things could always be fixed.
Late nights meant terminals glowing,
fingers on the keyboard,
ideas turning into something real.
I knew my world.
I knew where I belonged.
Then curiosity made me join the server.
I wasn't searching for anything.
Just curious about people,
new voices, new faces,
a world outside my code.
But somewhere between
“hello” and “who are you?”
I lost a little piece of my peace.
I was good in Python,
but not in ex,
not in flirting,
not in understanding why people change.
I could debug a program,
but I couldn't debug a heart.
Now I'm somewhere in between—
surrounded by people,
yet strangely alone.
I've met new people.
I've made new friends.
And maybe they're wonderful.
But the feeling was different
with AzartX, Phisher, and IsDebug.
They weren't just usernames.
They were my dev companions,
my people from the same battlefield.
Different screens, different projects,
but somehow the same army. 🪖
We spoke in bugs, APIs, servers,
late-night fixes and broken builds.
There was comfort in knowing
someone else was awake
fighting the same invisible war.
Now I scroll through endless messages,
watch people connect in seconds,
and wonder why I still feel
like I'm standing outside the room.
Maybe that's the strange thing
about being surrounded by people—
loneliness doesn't always need silence.
Sometimes it needs a server full of voices.
I don't want to sink
into this deep ache.
I don't want to lose myself
trying to belong somewhere
I was never meant to stay.
Maybe I don't have to.
Maybe I can step back,
open my terminal,
write some Python,
break something,
fix it,
and remember who I was
before all this noise.
I was good in codes.
I was good in my world.
And perhaps this isn't being lost.
Perhaps this is just the limbo—
the space between
who I was,
who I met,
and who I'm becoming.
— Yogesh
